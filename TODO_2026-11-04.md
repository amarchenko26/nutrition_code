## 1. Ultra-processed (UPF) rule

**Today's rule** (clean/usda/build_usda_nova.py): a product is NOVA 4 if its USDA ingredient list carries
ONE marker of any kind (a non-kitchen substance or a cosmetic additive). Result: 70.8% of classified
products and 76.3% of panel spending on matched UPCs are NOVA 4; 22% of NOVA 4 products rest on a single
marker, and that marker is "natural flavor" in a third of them. Random sample of 50 NOVA 4 products with
their markers: scratchpad file nova4_sample_50.txt (2026-11-03 session). Two regex errors to fix in any
case: "non-hydrogenated" matches the hydrogenated-oil pattern (895 products); "to protect flavor(s)"
matches the flavor pattern (7,599 products whose only flavor hit is such a phrase).

**Alternative marker rules** (products / spending in NOVA 4): two or more markers 55.3 / 62.5; drop
single weak markers (flavor, color, lecithin, pectin-xanthan-guar-agar alone) 59.6 / 66.9; rule D = one
non-kitchen substance (corn syrup, maltodextrin or dextrose, hydrogenated oil, protein isolate or
concentrate, modified starch, mechanically separated meat) OR two or more cosmetic additives 57.6 / 64.5;
rule E = non-kitchen OR three or more cosmetic 49.9 / 57.2; non-kitchen substances only 43.5 / 49.7.
Published NHANES-based estimates (Juul et al. 2022) put UPF at 57-60% of US calories, by food code.

**Anya's verdicts on examples** (2026-11-03): cookies = UPF, dill-pickle potato chips = UPF, bacon = UPF,
shredded cheese = not, frozen peas in butter sauce = not. These follow the food's category, not its
additive count. That is how the NHANES literature classifies (by food code: snacks, sweets, cured
meats, soft drinks, ready meals, cereal = 4; cheese, vegetables, fresh meat, milk, eggs, staples = 1-3).

**Proposal**: classify by Nielsen product module first, ingredient list second.
  1. Hand table over the 634 food modules (build/health/build_upf_modules.py, a SCORES-style dict as in
     build/prep_time/build_prep_time_index.py, with a rationale per module): class 1 / 2 / 3 / 4, or M
     (mixed: 3 or 4 decided by the label). Expected M modules: bread, buns, rolls, bagels, hot cereal,
     granola, yogurt, peanut butter, spaghetti and salsa sauces, soy sauce, pizza sauce, mayonnaise,
     tortillas, canned soup, baked beans, refried beans, nuts, dried fruit and snacks, sweet pickles,
     relish, popcorn kernels, trail mix, cranberry drinks, chocolate chips, breading, deli salads,
     refrigerated seafood, canned corned beef. Anya edits the table.
  2. Inside M modules apply rule D with the regex fixes (else the current 1/2/3 logic).
  3. UPC dictionary (build/health/build_upc_dictionary.py -> interim/health/upc_dictionary.parquet): every
     food UPC in products.tsv with its module class, label class, final class, and (see item 2 below)
     calorie density tier. Unlabelled UPCs in fixed-class modules get the module class, so the UPF share
     covers ALL calories, not only labelled ones; unlabelled UPCs in M modules are "unknown" (allocate
     by the module's labelled share, or show as a slice).
  4. Rerun build_nova_calorie_panel, build_module_price_calories, figure_nutrition_over_time,
     build_upf_consumers, figure_upf_products, figure_price_per_calorie_upf, analyze_variety_price_index
     (Fig 2d: module class instead of the median split of the marker share; M modules by the majority of
     their labelled spending).

## 2. Calorie imputation (calories per person) — DONE 2026-11-04 (build/health/build_upc_dictionary.py; both calorie builders rebuilt with the dictionary and the multipack count)

**Problem**: the labelled-only series (figs/health/diet/calories_over_time.png since 2026-11-04) rises from
450 to 835 kcal per adult equivalent per day over 2004-2020 because the USDA file is a 2024 snapshot and
the labelled share of calories rises from 39% to 81%; the series is a match artifact. The module x year
density imputation (printed by figure_nutrition_over_time.py, no longer plotted) gives 1,158 -> 930 ->
1,032 and Anya does not want it: year-specific module densities jump with the labelled set (powdered soft
drinks 171 -> 316 kcal/100 g between 2004 and 2005; the module-level price-per-calorie figures now use each
module's density pooled over 2004-2020, which removed the 2004 drop of the little-preparation line).

**Proposal**: impute at the UPC level, once, time-invariant:
  tier 1 the UPC's own USDA label; tier 2 the labelled UPC in the same brand x module x size unit with
  the closest package size (same product line, other flavor); tier 3 the brand x module mean density;
  tier 4 the module density pooled over all years. Report the share of calories in each tier by year.
  Then the trend reflects only what was bought. Also multiply grams by `multi` (multipack count; omitted
  today in build_nova_calorie_panel, build_module_price_calories, build_hi_usda_panel, build_hi_panel,
  build_hei_panel; +4.4% of labelled calories in 2015, soft drinks and yogurt). Expected level about
  1,000-1,100 kcal per adult equivalent per day: see the NHANES store-calorie comparison
  (figs/health/diet/calories_nhanes.png) for the external benchmark.
  Count-sized rows (units CT, PO; 9% of spending) still carry no grams; eggs are the main loss.

## 3. Preparation time, finer than the module

Today: one hand score per module (1-9), 110 of 685 modules unscored. Options: (a) score the USDA
branded_food_category (about 350 categories, e.g. "Frozen Dinners & Entrees" vs "Frozen Vegetables"),
assign at the UPC level where matched; (b) keyword flags in the Nielsen upc_descr (RTE, INSTANT, MICROWAVE,
MIX, KIT, FROZEN ENTREE, PRECUT, SLICED, SHREDDED) to split modules; (c) both, with the module score as the
fallback. ATUS by income and education (cooking_time_by_group.png) waits for the two microdata zips in
raw/atus/ (see raw/atus/README.txt), then clean/atus/clean_atus.py (untested) and figure_cooking_time.py.
The 2015 kink in figs/health/prep_time/prep_time_trend.png (left) and the 2015 slope change of the price
index are commodity-price cycles in the spending weights (eggs 2015-16, milk, beef; details in CLAUDE.md, RESULTS
2026-11-04). Options: weight the prep score by units or calories instead of spending, or hold prices at 2014
levels (needs the UPC-level RMS files with total_units, on Oscar); for the price figures, a robustness panel that
excludes eggs and milk, or a cap on any module's weight within a healthiness group.

## 4. Healthiness definition (paused since 2026-11-03)

Open question from the Prices section: how to split products by healthiness from the USDA labels rather
than by a fixed module score, and whether to recompute a module's healthiness every year. Candidates: a
nutrient-profile score per UPC from the label (sugar, saturated fat, sodium, fiber per 100 g or per 1,000
kcal, e.g. the Allcott HI formula or the UK FSA nutrient profiling model), aggregated to module x year by
spending; HEI stays the diet-level outcome. Touches analyze/variety/analyze_variety_price_index.py
(HI_CUTS groups) and build/variety/build_module_healthiness.py.
